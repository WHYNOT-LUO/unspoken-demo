## A familiar scene

Every time a child spends money, the parents ask: "Where did the money go? What did you buy? Why are you spending again?"

To the parents, this is care, and a way of teaching the child to manage money. But to the child, every purchase seems to come with a "package", and inside it is **guilt**: as if spending money were wrong in itself.

## One situation, two experiences

| | What the parent is thinking | What the child may feel |
|---|---|---|
| Asking where the money went | I care about you; I'm afraid you'll waste it or be cheated | I'm not trusted; spending money means I did something wrong |
| Repeating "money doesn't come easily" | I want you to value things | I'm a burden on my family |

Neither side means any harm, but the love gets "bent out of shape" on the way.

## For parents

- **Say what you're worried about, instead of only asking questions**: "I'm a bit worried you might get scammed online. Can we talk about it?"
- **Set rules together, instead of checking afterwards**: for example, your child decides how to use their monthly pocket money, and bigger purchases are discussed in advance.
- **Allow small mistakes**: wasting a little money is part of learning to manage it.
- **Watch your tone**: the same question asked with curiosity and asked with blame sounds completely different to a child.

## For children

- Try to understand that your parents' questions often come from worry.
- Say how you feel, calmly: "Every time you ask like that, I feel like I've done something wrong, and it upsets me."
- Suggest a solution: "Could we set some rules for my pocket money? Then you won't have to worry, and I won't have to explain every time."

## Reflection

- **Children**: When did you last feel guilty about spending money? What did you most wish your parents had said?
- **Parents**: When you ask your child about their spending, what are you really worried about? How could you say that worry directly?
