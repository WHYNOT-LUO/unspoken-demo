## You always have the right to say "no"

In any relationship, whether with a boyfriend or girlfriend, a friend, or someone you met online, you have the right to:

- refuse anything you don't want to do, including physical contact;
- change your mind, even if you agreed before;
- keep your own privacy, friends and interests;
- leave a relationship that makes you uncomfortable, at any time.

**Someone who truly cares about you will respect your "no".**

## What a healthy relationship looks like

- You feel relaxed and safe around the other person.
- You can disagree without being afraid.
- The other person supports you staying in touch with friends and family.
- When something upsets you, you can talk about it calmly.

## Warning signs

Take these seriously if they happen:

- the other person often checks your phone, or limits who you can see;
- they say things like "if you don't do this, you don't love me";
- they ask you to keep secrets from your parents or teachers;
- they ask you for private photos, or touch you in ways that make you uncomfortable;
- they threaten you, humiliate you, or make you feel afraid.

None of this **is love**. It is control or harm. It is not your fault.

## How to say no

- Keep it short and clear: "I don't want to." "I'm not willing."
- You don't need to give lots of reasons, and you don't need to apologise.
- If they keep pushing, leave, and tell an adult you trust.

## If you're in danger or get hurt

- **Tell an adult you trust as soon as possible**: a parent, a teacher or your school counsellor.
- If you are in immediate danger, **call 110 right away**.
- Don't hide it because you're afraid of being blamed. Protecting yourself matters more than anything.

The law gives minors special protection. Being hurt is not your fault, and you deserve help.

## Reflection

- Write down one sentence you could use to say no to someone, and say it out loud a few times.
- If something made you uncomfortable, who would you most want to tell first?
