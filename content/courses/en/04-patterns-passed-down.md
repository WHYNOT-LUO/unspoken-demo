## A familiar feeling

Many parents have had this moment: the words come out, and suddenly you realise they are exactly what you hated hearing as a child.

Much of the way we treat our children is learned from our own parents. However we were treated when we were young, we tend to treat the next generation the same way, even if it hurt us at the time.

This is nobody's fault. It is the first "way of loving" we learned, and the one we know best.

## Seeing the pattern is where change begins

You don't need to change everything at once. Start by trying to **notice**:

- What are the few things I say to my child most often?
- Which of them did I also hear a lot as a child?
- How did I feel when I heard them back then?

Once we can see a pattern, we have a choice: we can carry on, or we can try something different.

## Breaking the pattern: small ways to do it differently

- **Pause first**: when emotions rise, give yourself a few seconds before deciding what to say.
- **Describe instead of blaming**: replace "Why are you always like this?" with "I see the homework isn't finished yet, and I'm feeling anxious."
- **Ask about your child's feelings**: "Just now, did you feel like I didn't trust you?"
- **Allow yourself to be imperfect**: no one can drop habits built over many years all at once.

## What to do after saying something hurtful

Repairing matters as much as not making mistakes. A parent can say:

> "I was too worked up just now and spoke harshly. I'm sorry. I was actually worried about you."

A sincere apology shows your child that when something goes wrong in a relationship, it can be repaired. It also shows them how to face their own mistakes.

## Reflection

- Write down one thing you hated hearing as a child, but now notice yourself saying too.
- If you expressed the same worry in a different way, what would you say?
